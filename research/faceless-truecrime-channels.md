# What the big faceless true-crime / mystery channels do that we don't

Researched 2026-09-27. Scope: the most-followed faceless channels in true crime, mystery, dark history and unsolved cases, compared with our pipeline (`pipeline/STYLE.md`, `research/captions-style.md`, `research/platform-standards.md`, `research/elisa-reference-analysis.md`) and our latest video (`topics/phantom-of-heilbronn`, v4: 83.4 s, 195 words, review sheets 01/05/07, `cover.jpg`, `publish.md`).

Legend: **[S]** comes from a primary observation (channel data pulled with yt-dlp/RSS, video transcripts from YouTube's transcript panel, screenshots of channel pages). **[S2]** comes from secondary coverage (press, wikis, vendor blogs). **[I]** is my inference.

Method and limits:
- Subscriber counts come from yt-dlp `channel_follower_count` on 2026-09-27. Short-view counts come from each channel's `/shorts` tab. Upload cadence comes from the channel RSS feed (the last 15 uploads).
- Transcripts come from YouTube's own transcript panel (auto-captions), viewed in the browser. yt-dlp caption downloads were rate-limited (HTTP 429 / "confirm you're not a bot") after the first batch.
- Words per minute (wpm) = transcript words ÷ full runtime. This includes pauses, so it is comparable to our figure (195 words ÷ 83.4 s = **140 wpm**).
- Video playback was blocked by the same rate limit. Visual findings therefore come from Shorts cover/poster frames on channel grids, not from frame-by-frame viewing. Treat per-shot claims (cut rate, zooms) as unverified.
- **TikTok counts could not be verified.** tiktok.com served a bot-check page, which I did not try to bypass. The only TikTok number found is secondary (Mr. Nightmare, below). Instagram was not checked.

**Key structural finding [S]:** the big faceless long-form channels post **very few Shorts** (Scary Interesting 9, Coffeehouse 9, Mr. Nightmare 10, EWU 12). The Shorts that reach millions fall into two groups:
- **Real-footage clips with subtitles** (EWU, Catching Killers).
- **Very fast-told micro-stories** (Scary Interesting).

The channels that mass-produce narrated case summaries (Criminally Listed, 226 Shorts, median ~6K; Chills, 400+, median ~16K) do poorly. Our format sits between these two groups.

## 2. Findings by dimension

### 2.1 Hook (first 1–3 s)
Opening words of the top Shorts [S, transcripts]:

| Short (views) | First words | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Scary Interesting "Road Block" (5.7M) | "A man was driving from 29 Palms California to Albuquerque…" | Protagonist + motion + place. The threat arrives within ~15 s |
| Scary Interesting "Don't Look Back…" (1.2M) | "In 1987 a man was driving… when the brakes on his car suddenly failed" | Incident inside the first sentence (~4 s) |
| EWU "The Most Chilling Interrogation…" (2.9M) | Real audio: the detective's question about laughing while killing | The strongest real line from the case, first |
| Catching Killers "Father Realizes…" (40M) | Real audio: "we got a tip from the FBI…" | Real audio, cold |
| Coffeehouse "These Unexplained Deaths…" (266K) | "one of the most terrifying stories to exist and to this day nobody knows…" | Superlative + an unresolved promise, then the date |
| Coffeehouse "Google Earth's DARK Secret 2" (223K) | "What if I told you that you'd just walked past a genuine serial killer" | Direct "you" question |
| Chills "True Story: Christine Walters" | "It's the middle of the night and a woman is pounding on a stranger's door" | Present-tense scene |
| Criminally Listed "This father got vengeance…" (43K) | "This father went to extreme lengths to avenge his daughter's murder." | Thesis sentence. Lowest performer |

- The winners open **on a person doing something, or on real audio**, not on a claim about the case [S]. Our Phantom v4 opens on a claim ("For sixteen years, one woman left her DNA at forty crime scenes…"). That is closer to the Coffeehouse "superlative promise" style, which works but in the low hundreds of thousands [I].
- Real-footage channels put **the single most shocking real line in second 0** [S].

### 2.2 Narration pace and voice
- **wpm (words ÷ runtime)** [S]:
  - Scary Interesting: 238 words in 55 s = **~260 wpm**; 259 words in ~56 s = **~275 wpm**.
  - Coffeehouse Short: 210 in 60 s = **~210**; 190 in 57 s = **~200**.
  - Chills: 212 in 60 s = **~212**.
  - Criminally Listed (low performer): ~304+ in 118 s = **~155**.
  - **Phantom v4: 195 in 83.4 s = 140 wpm.** Our config caps scripts at 195 words.
- Scary Interesting reads as one breathless run-on story ("…and then what does he hear behind him…") with almost no pauses [S]. Our STYLE mandates 500–1200 ms pauses at scene changes and on up to 3 beats, which adds several seconds of silence [S, `pipeline/STYLE.md` §4].
- **Every top channel uses a human voice.** Two public cautionary cases:
  - JCS posted an AI-narrated video in Dec 2024, deleted it after backlash, and apologised; viewers said "AI voices were odd" [S2](https://www.dexerto.com/tv-movies/jcs-apology-true-crime-ai-controversy-explained-3179183/).
  - Mystery Recapped replaced TTS with a human "to avoid demonetization" [S2](https://youtube.fandom.com/wiki/Mystery_Recapped).
  - We already use ElevenLabs "Roger" and disclose it. The risk is the "stock AI voice" read (STYLE §4 already notes Brian was recognised) [I].
- Narrators speak in first person and with opinions: "Here's the part that gets me" (Chills) [S]; "this is obviously my attempt to piece together…" (Barely Sociable) [S]; EWU's closing jab, "sounds like he wants to forget his child just died" [S]. Our narration is an impersonal third-person voice by rule [I].

### 2.3 Titles and covers
- **Titles** [S]:
  - Scary Interesting: 2–3 cryptic words ("Road Block", "Uninvited Guest", "Don't Look Back…").
  - EWU: the formula "When [villain] Realize(s) They've Been Caught" (4.6M, 4.5M, 2.2M).
  - Mr. Nightmare: the payoff in the title ("Her twin brothers were the original victims 😨").
  - Coffeehouse: numbered series ("Google Earth's DARK Secret / 2 / 3") and a question ("…Was this murder?").
  - Ours is a 12-word sentence that spoils the reveal ("…a woman who never existed").
- **Title testing:** on the same day, yt-dlp listed a Coffeehouse upload as "The Horrifying Case of Jo Samak" and the watch page showed "The Moment He Realized His Murder Was Exposed... By His Child." [S]. This is likely YouTube's title A/B test [I].
- **Covers** (channel grids) [S]:
  - Coffeehouse: one real evidence image, **a red circle or red box drawn on the key detail** (a person in a Street View crowd, a shape in a pool), and 1–3 words in condensed caps with **one red keyword** ("THE **BONES**", "WAS THIS **MURDER**").
  - EWU: a suspect's face mid-reaction plus one quoted subtitle line.
  - Mr. Nightmare: a moody cinematic still plus a small subtitle in a red box.
  - Scary Interesting: the poster frame is the mid-video caption frame.
  - LEMMiNO long-form: flat illustrations with **one red element** and no faces.
- Our `cover.jpg` is frame 1: the hook box, a live caption ("FOR SIXTEEN YEARS,") and a SOURCE line. That is three text layers and no focal annotation [S].

### 2.4 Visual language
- **Evidence annotation:** Coffeehouse draws circles/boxes on Street View, CCTV and aerials and uses "Google Earth" framing as a recurring device [S]; Stewart names "aerial maps on Google" and CCTV/bodycam as core material [S2](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/01/18/creators-on-the-rise-coffeehouse-crime/). We have a marker `highlight` sweep and a map scene (`src/short/ShortVideo.tsx`, `scenes.tsx`). We have no circle/box callout on a photo and no Street-View / satellite "fly-in" [S, code grep].
- **Speaker-labelled subtitles on real audio:** Catching Killers tags the speaker above the line ("Suspect", "Officer 1", "Store owner") and writes stage directions ("\*KICKS WALL\*(X2)", "\*CRYING\*"); EWU captions quotes on real footage [S]. This applies to us whenever we have 911/interrogation/court audio [I].
- **Stock or AI atmosphere instead of evidence:** Scary Interesting and Mr. Nightmare Shorts illustrate the story with generic cinematic footage (a man on a desert road, coyotes, a dark hallway) [S]. Their top Short (5.7M) has no case evidence at all. Our policy (real evidence plus a SOURCE line on every shot) is more credible but denser. We already rule out AI people of real victims (STYLE §9).
- **Captions:**
  - Scary Interesting and Mr. Nightmare use **sentence-length captions** (6–10 words, smaller type, red keywords, or a red box) [S].
  - Coffeehouse and our video use 2–4-word caps pages.
  - EWU and Catching Killers use standard subtitles with speaker labels.
  - Nobody else stacks a SOURCE credit line on every shot. That is unique to us [S].

### 2.5 Music and sound
- LEMMiNO composes every score; "Cipher" alone has 6.1M views on LEMMiNO Music [S]. Nexpo layers projector clicks, static and ticking under calm narration [S2](https://grokipedia.com/page/Nexpo).
- Barely Sociable runs a **~39 s music-only cold open** before the first word [S, Pemberton transcript: music markers 0:06–0:39].
- We use one Kevin MacLeod bed per video. It is claim-prone and overused (platform-standards §3), and it is not a sound anyone associates with us [I].

### 2.6 Reveal / climax signalling
- Spoken pivots [S]: "and then what does he hear behind him" (Scary Interesting); "but it wasn't until the camera was analysed that things became very very concerning" (Coffeehouse); "Here's the part that gets me" (Chills).
- These pivots come **~5–10 s before the end**, not at 65–85 %. Shorts of 55–60 s turn in the last fifth [S].
- We already have a signpost allow-list (STYLE §2).

### 2.7 Endings, loops, CTAs
- **Stop on the image, no wrap-up** (Scary Interesting) [S]: "…didn't let up until he reached the I-40" (the opening line named the I-40 as the destination); "there's no telling what might have happened"; "Charlie's fears may have been justified". No CTA, no card. The last word lands and the Short restarts.
- **Echo loop** (Coffeehouse) [S]: the last line mirrors the first. The opening "nobody knows what happened" becomes the closing "no one knows who or what killed them". The opening "how would you react to this revelation" becomes the closing "how would you react if you suddenly found out", which doubles as a comment prompt.
- **Widen + route** (Chills) [S]: "Christine wasn't the only one. In that same area, five women have vanished. Full story on Chills." It adds a second mystery at the end and points to long-form.
- **Cliffhanger clip** (Mr. Nightmare) [S]: the Short starts mid-sentence and ends mid-sentence ("the diary entry truly painted the terrifying picture of").
- **Branded sign-off** (Barely Sociable long-form) [S]: "this is Barely Sociable and have a good night", plus a question to viewers.
- Why it matters: since 2025, Shorts count every replay as a view, and replay rate is a strong distribution signal [S2](https://nealschaffer.com/youtube-shorts-looping/) (vendor claims, but consistent). Phantom v4 ends with ~3 s of a silent name card (80.5–83.4 s on sheet_07). That is dead air right where the loop should restart [S/I].

### 2.8 Series, branding, cadence
- **Numbered series** [S]:
  - Nexpo: "The Dark Side of Reddit [2]", "Disturbing Things From Around the Internet" (15+ volumes).
  - Lazy Masquerade: "Disturbing Things I Found on the Internet [Vol.19]".
  - Coffeehouse: "Google Earth's DARK Secret 2 / 3".
  - Criminally Listed: "…Part 2", "10th Anniversary Edition".
  - Mr. Nightmare: the fixed template "3 Terrifying TRUE ___ Horror Stories".
  - Chills: "15 Scariest…".
- **Burned-in or overlaid brand** [S]:
  - A "SCARY INTERESTING" wordmark is burned into the bottom of its Shorts.
  - Coffeehouse shows its stamp logo as a player watermark.
  - Mr. Nightmare and Coffeehouse banners promise "Videos Weekly" / "…Weekly".
- **Cadence** [S, RSS]:
  - Weekly or more for the scaled channels: Chills ~3/wk, Scary Interesting and Mr. Nightmare ~1.6/wk, Criminally Listed ~1.3/wk, Coffeehouse ~1/wk; Catching Killers posts a Short every ~2 days.
  - Prestige channels post rarely: LEMMiNO ~2/yr, JCS and Barely Sociable a few per year.
  - Either you are the weekly habit or the rare event. Both groups are consistently one or the other [I].
- Our STYLE §1 forbids any title card, logo sting or brand beat. Nothing in our frames identifies the channel [S].

### 2.9 Victims and ethics
- Coffeehouse's stated policy is to "focus on the victim's perspective" and "pay homage to the victims" [S2](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/01/18/creators-on-the-rise-coffeehouse-crime/). Barely Sociable ends on the families: "two men are still missing… both families are still without any answers" [S].
- Counter-examples not to copy [S]:
  - EWU puts a child victim's photo on a Short cover ("Cali's death").
  - EWU titles call subjects "Evil".
  - Catching Killers' description sells "raw, uncensored footage" on Patreon.
- Our rules (no death imagery, dignity, a name card) are already stricter than the field. Keep them.

### 2.10 Community
- Mr. Nightmare runs on viewer-submitted stories ("Submitted by: Sabrina G." on a cover) [S].
- Coffeehouse polls its audience every 3–4 months to pick cases [S2](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/01/18/creators-on-the-rise-coffeehouse-crime/).
- The Shorts checked (Coffeehouse, Scary Interesting) had **no pinned creator comment** [S]; the top comment is viewer outrage ("these victims get no justice").
- Every channel pushes Patreon [S].
- Our `publish.md` lists sources "for pinned comment" but produces no ready-to-paste text [S].
