# The Elisa Lam Case

[VISUAL: 02_elisa_lam_portrait.png | 09_elisa_lam_lapd_photo.jpg]
For nineteen days, guests at the Cecil Hotel in downtown Los Angeles drank the water, showered in it, and made their morning coffee with it — without knowing a decomposing body was floating in the tank above them. That body belonged to Elisa Lam.

[VISUAL: 03_elisa_lam_student_photo.jpg | 06_elisa_lam_graduation.jpg]
Elisa was twenty-one, a University of British Columbia student from Vancouver traveling solo down the California coast. She called her parents every single day. She kept a Tumblr blog where she wrote honestly about her struggles with depression. She was buying gifts for her family at a downtown bookstore on the last day anyone saw her alive.

[VISUAL: 04_cecil_hotel_exterior.jpg | 07_cecil_hotel_los_angeles.jpg | 14_cecil_hotel_cbs_exterior.jpg]
She was staying at the Cecil Hotel — a fifteen-story building on Skid Row with a documented history of at least sixteen non-natural deaths, and a former address of both Richard Ramirez and serial killer Jack Unterweger.

[VISUAL: 01_elevator_surveillance_footage.mp4]
On January 31st, 2013, hotel surveillance captured Elisa inside an elevator. For two and a half minutes she pressed every button, hid in the corner, and peered around the door frame as if watching for someone who wasn't there. The doors stayed open for an unexplained amount of time. Tens of millions of people have studied this footage and no one agrees on what it means.

[VISUAL: 11_elevator_surveillance_still.jpg | 12_elevator_hiding_still.jpg]
Here is the detail that doesn't fit: that same afternoon, a bookstore manager half a block away described Elisa as outgoing and cheerful, buying gifts for her family. Two versions of the same person, same day, same hour.

[VISUAL: 05_elisa_lam_water_tank.jpg | 10_elevator_cctv_archive.mp4]
The LAPD searched the Cecil multiple times with dogs. She was fifteen floors above them the entire time. To reach the rooftop tank herself, she would have had to bypass an alarmed door, climb a ten-foot ladder, and lift a twenty-pound hatch — during what investigators believe was a severe episode of untreated bipolar disorder. Police say the hatch was closed when the body was found. The maintenance worker who found her says it was open.

[VISUAL: 08_nbc_news_body_found.mp4]
Discovery only came because guests complained about the water pressure and a strange taste. Nineteen days after she vanished, a maintenance worker climbed to the roof and found her — naked, floating face-up, approximately twelve inches from the top of the tank.

[VISUAL: 13_elisa_lam_cbs_portrait.jpg]
The coroner ruled accidental drowning. No trauma, no foul play. But Elisa's Tumblr kept posting for weeks after her death — scheduled entries she had written herself, words from a woman who could not have known they would be her last. The case was officially closed in 2013. The questions have never been.
