# Elisa Lam — The Water

[VISUAL: 08_nbc_news_body_found.mp4]
Guests at the Cecil Hotel kept complaining about the water — it tasted wrong. When maintenance finally opened a rooftop tank, they found why. A naked, decomposing body, twelve inches from the top. For nineteen days, guests had been drinking her.

[VISUAL: 02_elisa_lam_portrait.png | 06_elisa_lam_graduation.jpg | 03_elisa_lam_student_photo.jpg]
Her name was Elisa Lam — 21, a student from Vancouver, traveling solo. She kept a blog about her depression. After she died, it kept posting, entries she had scheduled herself.

[VISUAL: 01_elevator_surveillance_footage.mp4]
The last footage of her alive is from January 31st. She presses every button, hides in the corner, peers around the door frame, makes slow inexplicable gestures. The doors won't close. Tens of millions have watched it and no one agrees on what they're seeing.

[VISUAL: 11_elevator_surveillance_still.jpg | 12_elevator_hiding_still.jpg | 09_elisa_lam_lapd_photo.jpg]
That same afternoon, one block away, a bookstore manager described her as lively, buying gifts for her family.

[VISUAL: 04_cecil_hotel_exterior.jpg | 14_cecil_hotel_cbs_exterior.jpg]
Police searched the Cecil with dogs and never found her. The rooftop's alarmed door never triggered.

[VISUAL: 05_elisa_lam_water_tank.jpg | 07_cecil_hotel_los_angeles.jpg]
To enter the tank, someone had to lift a twenty-pound hatch. Police said it was closed when found. The maintenance worker said it was open. Never resolved.

[VISUAL: 10_elevator_cctv_archive.mp4]
The coroner ruled accidental drowning. Bipolar disorder, medication not taken. The footage is still there, still looping, still without an answer.

[VISUAL: 13_elisa_lam_cbs_portrait.jpg]
She once wrote about wanting a life that felt like her own. The tank was fifteen floors above where her parents would eventually stand, waiting.
